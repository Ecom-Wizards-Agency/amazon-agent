# Worked example: lavender pillow spray

This example distills a reviewed drafting session, not a proven conversion lift.
Client identity, live exports and assets stay outside the runtime skill. Use the
supplied evidence for every new product.

## Situation

Facts establish a 75 ml pillow spray with lavender, patchouli and vetiver oils plus
chamomile extract. No alcohol is listed; vegan/cruelty-free positioning is brand
confirmed. Directions specify light application to pillows or bedding. No sleep
study, scent-duration test, blanket textile test or superiority evidence is supplied.

Primary POE queries concern pillow spray and bedtime. Scent is prominent in both
positive and negative feedback; snippets mention pleasant fragrance, disliked smell,
weak intensity and short persistence. Quality has a strong positive rating
association. An adjacent niche includes cleaning and insect-control uses.

## Reasoning worth reusing

| Evidence | Interpretation | Product answer and visual | Copy example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pillow-spray and bedtime queries | Likely comparing known solutions; bedtime relevance matters | Actual bottle in a recognizable evening setting | Lavender for your bedtime ritual. |
| Scent is praised and criticized | Buyers need to judge fragrance suitability | Selected actual oils/extracts; real sensory assessment if available | Lavender oil meets patchouli, vetiver and chamomile extract. |
| Intended use involves light application | Show how it fits the routine | Actual application onto unoccupied bedding | Lightly mist your pillow or bedding before bedtime. |
| Quality and value matter | Use specific facts | Real formulation and honest 75 ml scale | Essential oils with chamomile extract. |

Ingredient identity is a defensible first answer, not proof every buyer likes the
fragrance. A verified sensory description would be more informative. Do not infer
"subtle", "never overpowering", "all-night fragrance" or "better sleep" from what
disappointed category buyers wanted.

An ingredient summary that does not mention alcohol is not by itself an
alcohol-free specification. Use that absence claim when the product evidence
actually establishes it.

## Improvements to retain

- Give lifestyle and usage different jobs. If both only say "your nightly ritual",
  merge them or give the demonstration a real new fact.
- Travel, gifting and brand story earn a slot only when material to this brief.
  A small bottle alone does not justify a prominent travel image.
- Formulation and ethical attributes can be reasons to choose without being unique.
- This client's sleep-claim limits do not apply to every category. Preserve an
  actually substantiated benefit on another product.
- A category complaint is neither our product's defect nor proof we solved it.
  Adjacent insect-control themes do not belong in this product's gallery.
- Prefer fewer useful images over repeated lifestyle scenes and a generic closer.
  Meet a requested count with real additional questions, not repeated copy.

## Weak-to-strong revisions

| Weak approach | Better decision |
| --- | --- |
| "Premium quality for better nights" | State a verified formulation/operation fact; quality ratings do not prove sleep benefits. |
| Automatic "Struggling to sleep?" opening | Lead with the relevant pillow-spray experience for the inferred category-aware buyer. |
| "No overpowering scent" | Describe the real blend; use intensity language only with product evidence. |
| "All natural" | Name actual oils/extract and supported absence claims; the full formula has other ingredients. |
| Generic "Meet your brand" after several ritual images | Keep only if it resolves contents, variant, quantity or another unanswered question. |
