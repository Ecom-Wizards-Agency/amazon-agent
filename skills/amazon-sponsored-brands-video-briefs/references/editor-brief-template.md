# Editor brief template

Deliver one complete branded Google Doc per batch, with no cover page, in the client's existing Video Briefings folder. An editor should understand the requested work from this document alone. Internal research, claims provenance and review commentary are not appended.

Use the agreed mode. Production specifies the requested complete cuts. Concept testing specifies three openings plus one shared second half per concept. Do not add test variants to a commissioned single-video assignment.

## Structure

```
# <Client> <Product Line>: Sponsored Brands Video Briefing
<date, market, on-screen/VO language>
<exact product/ASIN/variant and deliverable count>
<how the requested cuts assemble>

## Global rules
### Sound-off design and audio
### Specs (verbatim, non-negotiable)
### Absolute do-not list

## Video 1: <objective or query cluster> (target NN s)
<desire / core avatar>
<keywords and awareness, with a short reason>
### Timeline
| Card | Time | On-screen text (final) | Visual direction |
|---|---|---|---|

## Brand and finishing direction
<actual pack, fonts, colors, wordmark rules, output filename>

## Supplied assets (optional)
<verified useful links or a precise known production gap>
```

One production Video section describes one complete cut. Repeat it only for requested additional cuts. For concept testing replace its Timeline with:

```
### Part 1: the three angles
#### Angle 1: <buying criterion>
<who this opening is for>
| Card | Time | On-screen text (final) | Visual direction |
|---|---|---|---|
#### Angle 2: <different buying criterion>
<own table>
#### Angle 3: <different buying criterion>
<own table>
### Part 2: shared second half
<one table, identical in every cut of this concept>
```

## Header and deliverables

State the date, marketplace and language once. Identify the exact ASIN, pack and size when these affect execution. State the number of finished cuts and their duration. For a test explain that one cut is one opening plus the shared Part 2. Card rows are scenes within a cut, not extra videos. Language/export duplicates are named separately.

## Global direction

The complete message must work with sound off. Frame 1 shows the identifiable product already in action. No logo intro, fade-in, title card or empty introductory pack shot. Give specific audio instructions: whether VO exists, exact spoken wording or its relationship to the cards, pronunciation where needed, music and the final frame. Do not add unscripted spoken claims. No fixed VO or length applies to every assignment.

Use at most seven words per card, one card at a time, high contrast and safe margins. Check readability at approximately 300 pixels wide and keep text clear of player controls. Allow roughly one second per 12 characters plus a visual beat, at least 2.5 seconds for a visible card. Extend a slot rather than shrink text. Translations may need longer timing.

Retain this SBV specs box verbatim after verifying the requested placement's current requirements:

> 16:9, 1920x1080, MP4 or MOV, max 500 MB, target length per video (Amazon max 45 s). Audio track required at minimum 96 kbps even though default playback is muted. No letterboxing or pillarboxing. No black or blank frames at start or end. No URLs, social handles, QR codes. No price, discount or urgency text. No competitor names or packs on screen. All on-screen text in the marketplace language.

The absolute do-not list is short and specific to the product and assignment. Include relevant claim/imagery bans and the shortcuts an editor could accidentally introduce: an old pack, a misleading demonstration, unreviewed packaging text or an implied transformation. Explain ambiguous wording in context. Final visible and spoken copy is fixed; the editor corrects typos and layout, not claims.

## Per-video context and timeline

State the shopper's intent and awareness in a few sentences, using the assignment's exact queries where supplied. Distinguish branded recognition from non-branded comparison. Do not paste research tables or the internal shortlist rationale.

Every script uses this table:

| Card | Time | On-screen text (final) | Visual direction |
|---|---|---|---|

Use continuous intervals such as `0–3.5 s`; a production cut starts at zero and ends at its target. Each test opening starts at zero and joins Part 2 at the same time. Card IDs must be unique within each table sequence. The validator's Video heading uses `(target NN s)`.

Visual directions describe the intended result, framing, action and product truth. They do not identify source clips or assume footage availability. Be specific enough to film or select a shot without inventing a mechanism, outcome, label or testimonial. If exact supplied footage is useful, link it in a compact asset section and identify its role.

The final card is textless, marked `[No added text]`: hold the real pack/wordmark as appropriate and describe a clean restart into the opening. No added CTA or unscripted final claim.

## Concept-testing differences

Each opening leads on a distinct buying criterion, not a synonym of the same claim. Give the three angles short names and separate tables. List the shared second half once. Keep it identical in all three cuts and avoid repeating a promise already spent in the opening. Repeat this structure per confirmed concept, not a single set of three angles for the whole batch.

## Brand, assets and gaps

Put the brand decisions needed for editing in the briefing: actual fonts and colors, pack identity and proportions, wordmark/clear-space rules, lighting and texture, output filename(s). Use the verified brand kit rather than generic styling.

Supplied-asset links are optional. When included, give source, intended use, suitability/crop instruction and actual rights status; never infer rights. A known missing required shot gets a precise production instruction. Unknown footage availability is stated as unknown and does not justify an invented source or a fake footage request.

## Languages and internal QA

For additional languages, add a compact translation table outside the Video sections: card number and text per language. State the master. Use local query terminology, preserve claim decisions and adjust duration when needed.

Before delivery, trace every final visible/VO line and visual implication internally, resolve medium/high claims risk, run the mechanical validator and independent review, and inspect the native pages. Deliver no claims appendix, campaign plan, thresholds, performance method or full research dossier.
