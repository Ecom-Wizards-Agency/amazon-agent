## Question

A brand asked the agency to review new listing copy for a microneedling facial kit for compliance. The draft used 'Doctor Recommended', 'safe and painless', 'reducing the appearance of wrinkles and fine lines', a needle length, and microneedling terms.

## Answer

Before launching copy for a skincare device that touches the skin with needles, remove unsubstantiated endorsement and safety claims, anti-aging claims without approved evidence, any needle length of 0.3 mm or more, and microneedling terms unless the device has an FDA 510(k) clearance. Any of these can make Amazon classify the item as a regulated medical device and remove the listing.

## Cause

Several phrases in the draft are restricted. Endorsement claims such as 'Doctor Recommended' need third-party substantiation. 'Safe' and 'painless' are unsupported safety claims. Anti-aging claims need approved evidence. Microneedling procedure terms, or a needle length of 0.3 mm or more, make Amazon classify a skin roller as a medical device that needs an FDA 510(k) number.

## Fix

1. Remove endorsement claims such as 'Doctor Recommended' unless a named, dated third-party source substantiates them.
2. Remove 'safe' and 'painless' style safety claims.
3. Treat 'reducing the appearance of wrinkles and fine lines' as an anti-aging claim; keep it out unless clinical evidence exists and Amazon approves it.
4. Remove any needle length of 0.3 mm or more from copy, images, search terms and A+ content.
5. Use microneedling and needling terms only if the device has an FDA 510(k) clearance.
6. Have the brand approve the revised copy, then implement it (approval gate).

## Verify

Revised title, bullets, backend terms, images and A+ content contain none of the listed phrases and no needle length of 0.3 mm or more, and the listing shows no policy warning after the change.

## Stop before

Stop before any submission, appeal, case send, refund, price change or listing change unless the operator approved that exact action.
