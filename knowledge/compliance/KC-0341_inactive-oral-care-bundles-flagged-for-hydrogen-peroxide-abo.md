## Question

An inactive oral care bundle receives a warning that hydrogen peroxide exceeds 12%, while the supplier's SDS reportedly states a lower concentration. Should the seller ignore the notice or dispute it?

## Answer

Read the exact notice and deadline, then verify the SDS against the finished product and every component in the bundle. Amazon US guidance prohibits cosmetics containing more than 12% hydrogen peroxide; a lower verified concentration supports disputing that specific classification. Closing or deleting a listing does not itself remove an Account Health violation.

## Cause

The notice alleges a concentration restriction; the underlying trigger is not established. Do not assume which bundle component caused it, or that an inactive offer prevents compliance review.

## Fix

1. Verify seller and US marketplace, open the affected Account Health record and read Review details when offered. Follow the record's evidence requirements and deadline.
2. Check SDS identity and composition sections, chemical identity and final-product concentration. A generic document needs evidence linking it to the actual branded product.
3. Assemble original SDS, genuine packaging images for every bundle component and truthful manufacturer mapping where needed. Check bundle and standalone content for conflicting concentration claims.
4. If supported, prepare a dispute requesting manual review of the concentration classification. Use the notice's submission route; do not admit a concentration violation that the evidence does not establish.

## Verify

Verify Amazon's decision and resulting Account Health record. A submission receipt, inactive offer or disappearance from inventory alone does not establish resolution.

## Stop before

Stop before any submission, appeal, case send, refund, price change or listing change unless the operator approved that exact action.
