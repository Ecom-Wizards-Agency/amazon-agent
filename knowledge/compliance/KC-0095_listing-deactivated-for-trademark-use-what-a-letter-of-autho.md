## Question

Two licensed-product listings were deactivated for trademark use; the same letter of authorization reinstated one and was rejected for the other, with Amazon listing the documents it would accept.

## Answer

A trademark deactivation can be cleared three ways: a letter of authorization or licence, a retraction sent by the rights owner, or a detail page that uses the mark only descriptively. A letter is accepted only if it names the rights owner with address, names you exactly as on your selling account, and explicitly authorises use of the IP with your own brand name. Check those three points before you submit, and never use 'similar to' with another brand's mark.

## Cause

Amazon's reply lists the accepted remedies and the three elements a letter of authorization or licence must contain (rights owner name and address, seller name and address matching the selling account, explicit authorisation to use the IP with the seller's own brand name). The thread does not establish which element the second submission lacked, or why the same letter passed for the other listing.

## Fix

1. Read the rejection and note which of the three accepted remedies applies.
2. If using a letter or licence, check it names the rights owner and their address, names you with the exact name and address on your selling account, and explicitly authorises use of the IP with your own brand name.
3. Get a corrected letter from the rights owner if any element is missing.
4. Alternatively ask the rights owner to send a retraction to Amazon directly, using the contact in the deactivation notice.
5. Or edit the detail page so the trademark is used only descriptively ('compatible with', 'works with'), never as your brand or with 'similar to'.
6. Resubmit (operator approval before submitting).

## Verify

The listing returns to Active and the complaint clears in Account Health.

## Stop before

Stop before any submission, appeal, case send, refund, price change or listing change unless the operator approved that exact action.
