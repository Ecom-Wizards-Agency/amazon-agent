## Question

A cosmetic face patch listing was blocked because of wording on the packaging. Amazon asked for 510(k) and FDA documents, then, after keyword changes and approved photos, asked for a formal explanation with proof if the product does not need a 510(k).

## Answer

When Amazon flags a cosmetic product as a possible medical device because of packaging wording, expect it to ask for 510(k) and FDA documents and, if you claim no 510(k) is needed, for a formal exemption letter with attached proof rather than an email explanation. Change the flagged listing wording first and supply packaging photos. If the product is not core to the account, weigh a removal order against continuing the compliance review.

## Cause

Wording on the packaging and listing led Amazon to treat the cosmetic patch as a possible medical device needing FDA 510(k) clearance. The exact flagged wording lives in the case and is not in the thread.

## Fix

1. Read the full case to get the exact wording Amazon objects to.
2. Change the flagged keywords in the listing as Amazon recommends.
3. Send packaging photos when asked.
4. If the product is cosmetic and needs no 510(k), send a formal letter from the brand explaining why it is exempt, with supporting proof attached; an email or chat explanation alone was not accepted (operator approval required before submitting).
5. If the product is not essential, a removal order and closing the listing ends the review (operator approval required); units in reserved status can only be removed once they become available.

## Verify

Amazon accepts the exemption letter and the listing is reinstated, or the inventory is removed and the listing closed.

## Stop before

Stop before any submission, appeal, case send, refund, price change or listing change unless the operator approved that exact action.
