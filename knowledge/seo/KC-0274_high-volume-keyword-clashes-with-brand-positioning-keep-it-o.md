## Question

Keyword research put a high-volume category term into the title, but the brand markets against that category. The brand also asked why competitor titles are longer than the new draft.

## Answer

Write titles to the 75-character limit and use item highlights for the next 125 characters. Do not copy longer competitor titles; they risk automatic correction or dropping out of search. If a high-volume keyword conflicts with the brand positioning, keep it out of the title, mention it in the bullets for relevance and target it in ads. Choose one title positioning and test it with Search Query Performance CTR and conversion data instead of arguing from search volume alone.

## Cause

Titles are capped at 75 characters including spaces, and item highlights add up to 125 characters of comma-separated phrases shown below the title (first-party title guidelines). Longer competitor titles are listings not yet brought to the limit, and the help page warns that such titles may be corrected automatically or may not appear in search results. A term with search volume can still be covered for relevance outside the title.

## Fix

1. Draft the title within 75 characters, including spaces, using the highest-volume term that fits the brand positioning.
2. Put secondary attributes into item highlights (up to 125 characters, comma-separated phrases).
3. Keep the off-brand but relevant term in the bullets and target it with PPC instead of the title.
4. When two title positionings compete, choose one, then compare CTR and conversion rate against the market in Search Query Performance and adjust.

## Verify

The live title is at most 75 characters, item highlights show below it, and Search Query Performance shows impressions for the term moved to bullets and ads.

## Stop before

Stop before any submission, appeal, case send, refund, price change or listing change unless the operator approved that exact action.
