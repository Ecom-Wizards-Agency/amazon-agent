## Question

A client questioned an agency-written title and bullets, asked why certain feature phrases were included and whether a fabric name was invented, and proposed their own longer title packed with use cases and pain-relief terms, asking whether it would rank better.

## Answer

When a client challenges an optimized title, settle it with evidence rather than opinion: score both versions against the same keyword list and check them against Amazon's title rules. A longer title that stacks use cases and benefit claims usually breaks the length limit and can add claim risk without improving relevance. Concede wording that carries no keyword value and keep the version that scores higher.

## Cause

The client's title exceeded the title length guideline and scored lower on Ranking Juice against the master keyword list than the agency version; feature phrases with low search volume were included for product explanation, not search volume.

## Fix

1. Score both titles against the same master keyword list with Ranking Juice and share the comparison.
2. Check both against the title rules: 75 characters or fewer, extra detail goes to Item highlights.
3. Offer to drop low-value feature phrases or invented feature names the client does not want, as long as the score holds.
4. Flag any health or pain-relief wording in the client's version for a claims check before it is used.
5. Get the client's approval of the final version before pushing it live.

## Verify

The approved title is within the length limit and its Ranking Juice score is at least that of the alternative.

## Stop before

Stop before any submission, appeal, case send, refund, price change or listing change unless the operator approved that exact action.
