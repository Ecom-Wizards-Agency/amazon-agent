## Question

A brand that had just started merchant-fulfilled shipping through its 3PL saw a late shipment rate far above Amazon's 4% target in Account Health and asked what strategy to take.

## Answer

A late shipment rate above 4% needs both a Plan of Action and an operational fix, because the rate only falls as on-time orders replace late ones. Set the handling time to what the fulfilment partner can actually meet, have the partner confirm shipments on time daily, and move steady volume to FBA where possible. Describe in the Plan of Action only controls that are actually in place.

## Cause

The fulfilment partner confirmed shipments after the expected ship date on a large share of seller-fulfilled orders during the first weeks of the FBM program, so the late shipment rate exceeded the target.

## Fix

1. Open Account Health > Late Shipment Rate and note the rate, the target and the window.
2. Write a standard Plan of Action: cause (new fulfilment set-up, partner delays), corrective actions taken, and preventive controls such as daily monitoring of unshipped orders (operator approval before submitting).
3. Increase the handling time on the affected offers so the expected ship date matches what the partner can do.
4. Have the fulfilment partner confirm shipments on time every day and monitor it daily.
5. Move long-term volume to FBA so seller-fulfilled late shipments stop accruing.
6. Optionally call Seller Support to explain the start-up situation; in the thread it was treated as a warning.

## Verify

The late shipment rate in Account Health falls below 4% over the following windows as on-time shipments replace late ones.

## Stop before

Stop before any submission, appeal, case send, refund, price change or listing change unless the operator approved that exact action.
