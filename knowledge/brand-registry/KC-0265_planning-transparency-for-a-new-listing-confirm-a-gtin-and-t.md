## Question

A brand owner asked for an update on Transparency to stop hijackers on a new listing. The products had no EAN/UPC barcodes yet and the brand registration date was unclear.

## Answer

Before promising Transparency protection, confirm the product has a GTIN and check the brand's eligibility on the Transparency site, then get barcodes on the packaging before inbound. Transparency protects against hijackers and Buy Box loss, so for a large catalog weigh the per-unit labelling effort first. Until it is live, make the Amazon unit harder to copy with a distinct pack or an Amazon-only bundle extra.

## Cause

The agency lead said Transparency needs a product barcode (EAN/UPC) and a fully registered brand. The captured help page only says to check eligibility on the Transparency site, and the protection-roles page lists Transparency under the Rights Owner role. Until both prerequisites were in place, the agency could not set it up.

## Fix

1. Confirm whether the product will be listed with a GTIN (EAN/UPC from GS1). Decide before creating the listing, because it determines how the listing is set up.
2. Confirm the brand's Brand Registry status (registered or pending trademark) and check eligibility on the Transparency site. The 'fully registered' requirement is the agency lead's statement, not a captured rule.
3. Have the barcodes applied to the packaging before the units are sent to Amazon.
4. Until Transparency is live, make the Amazon unit harder to copy: use slightly different packaging for the Amazon version, or add a small Amazon-only extra to the bundle. A separate GTIN is valid only where the Amazon pack is a genuinely different product or bundle; do not issue a second barcode for an identical item.

## Verify

The Transparency eligibility check passes for the product and codes can be ordered for the GTIN.

## Stop before

Stop before any submission, appeal, case send, refund, price change or listing change unless the operator approved that exact action.
