## Question

The trademark owner did not control the brand's Brand Registry Administrator role and unknown users had access. The agency filed a dispute and waited for Brand Registry Support.

## Answer

If someone else holds the Administrator role, open a Brand Registry Support ownership request and prove the chain from the trademark registration to the requester, including corporate documents when a parent company owns the registrant. Once the role is back, audit User Permissions, confirm each unfamiliar user, and remove the unknown ones.

## Cause

Brand Registry roles can be held by users the rights owner does not control. Here, Brand Registry Support reviewed a dispute that included the trademark registration and corporate documents linking the requester to the registrant, then assigned the Administrator role to the requester's email.

## Fix

1. Request the role through the route that applies. If your account already has at least one enrolled brand, use Brand Registry Support with the topic Update brand ownership. Otherwise, submit a Brand Registry application with the same trademark number and appeal the brand's enrollment status. State which email should become Administrator. Sending needs operator approval.
2. Attach proof: the trademark registration, plus documents linking the requester to the registrant, such as a membership certificate showing the parent company owns the registrant entity.
3. After Support assigns the Administrator role, review User Permissions, identify every user, and remove the ones nobody recognizes. Confirm unfamiliar addresses (for example a law firm) before removing them.
4. Invite the agency's Brand Registry account as Administrator if it needs to manage the brand.

## Verify

Support's message confirms the Administrator assignment, and User Permissions lists only known users.

## Stop before

Stop before any submission, appeal, case send, refund, price change or listing change unless the operator approved that exact action.
