## Question

A hijacker appeared on a listing while the home-country trademark was stuck in examination over similarity to another mark, and the brand owner asked whether nothing could be done and whether a trademark filed in a faster country would be accepted by Amazon worldwide. In a sibling thread on another account, a brand owner flagged a fake listing on a second marketplace and the agency needed a trademark that covered that marketplace before it could request a takedown.

## Answer

Without Brand Registry you cannot use Report a Violation or Transparency against hijackers or counterfeits. Brand Registry accepts a registered trademark, or a pending one from an accepted office, in each country where you enroll; a pending mark can still be rejected when a similar brand already exists in Amazon's store. If your pending application does not qualify or is stuck, file in a faster accepted office, enroll with whichever mark qualifies first, then add the others. For a counterfeit on another marketplace, gather the trademark that covers that country before you report.

## Cause

Brand Registry enforcement tools (Report a Violation, Transparency) need the brand to be enrolled. Enrollment needs an active registered trademark, or a pending one from an accepted trademark office, in each country where you enroll. A pending mark is not enough when it comes from an office Amazon does not accept for pending marks, or when Amazon finds a similar brand already in its store. The thread did not establish which of these blocked enrollment; the agency treated the pending home application as unusable and looked for a faster registered mark elsewhere.

## Fix

1. Check the trademark status at the trademark office and whether that office is among those whose pending applications Brand Registry accepts.
2. If the pending mark does not qualify or enrollment is stuck, consider filing in a faster accepted office (the agency used a country with quicker registration) and enroll the brand in Brand Registry with whichever trademark qualifies first.
3. For a counterfeit on a specific marketplace, gather the trademark that covers that country (registered, or pending at an accepted office for that country) and add it to the brand under Manage > Manage intellectual property > Connect a trademark.
4. Once enrolled, report the offending listing or seller through Report a Violation when you have evidence of an IP violation (each report needs operator approval), and plan Transparency enrollment.
5. When a trademark moves from pending to registered, check its Trademark status under Manage intellectual property; if the change is not shown, contact Brand Support and choose Update your trademark status.

## Verify

The brand shows as enrolled in Brand Registry for the affected marketplace and Report a Violation accepts the report.

## Stop before

Stop before any submission, appeal, case send, refund, price change or listing change unless the operator approved that exact action.
