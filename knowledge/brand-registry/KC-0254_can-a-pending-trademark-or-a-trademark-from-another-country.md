## Question

A brand with trademark applications pending in many jurisdictions, and a home-country trademark from an ineligible origin, wanted Transparency to stop copycats. The owner asked whether a pending trademark already listed in Brand Registry was enough. In a sibling thread, a different owner asked whether a registered trademark from another country could be used for Transparency on a US listing.

## Answer

Do not plan Transparency on a pending trademark: Transparency needs a government-registered mark, while Brand Registry also accepts pending ones, so a brand can be in Brand Registry and still not qualify. Eligibility also depends on brand origin. The Amazon text quoted in the source thread listed the US, Canada, Germany, France, Italy, Spain, the UK, India, Japan and China and excluded Australia, but no local capture holds that list, so check the Transparency site before advising. Register the fastest eligible national mark rather than waiting on every application, and use the Brand Registry tools that accept the pending mark in the meantime.

## Cause

Transparency enrollment needs a government-registered trademark, and the Amazon text the agency lead quoted limits eligible brand origins to the US, Canada, Germany, France, Italy, Spain, the UK, India, Japan and China, with Australian-origin brands not eligible (though brands enrolled from an eligible origin are still protected in the Australia store). Brand Registry itself accepts pending trademarks in many countries, so a brand can be enrolled in Brand Registry yet still not qualify for Transparency.

## Fix

1. List every trademark the brand holds with its office and status; separate fully registered marks from pending applications and EU-wide marks from national ones.
2. Check the brand origin against the eligible-origin list on Amazon's Transparency eligibility page before planning on Transparency.
3. If no registered mark from an eligible origin exists, pursue the fastest national registration in an eligible country; the agency lead's experience is that a German national mark registers fastest, with a paid fast-track option that has no guaranteed processing time.
4. Once a mark is registered, add it to the brand in Brand Registry (Manage intellectual property) and confirm the Trademark status shows registered; contact Brand Support with Update your trademark status if it does not.
5. Then enroll in Transparency; meanwhile use Brand Registry tools that accept the pending mark.

## Verify

Brand Registry shows the eligible-origin trademark as registered, the current Transparency eligibility page lists that origin, and the Transparency enrollment accepts the brand.

## Stop before

Stop before any submission, appeal, case send, refund, price change or listing change unless the operator approved that exact action.
