## Question

A brand owner asked how quickly a new hijacker on their listing could be removed.

## Answer

When a hijacker appears, place a test buy immediately. A common-law trademark report (US, Canada and South Africa stores) cannot be submitted without the test-buy order ID, and the offer may vanish before you can buy. Report only if the received unit shows infringing or counterfeit goods, never a reseller of genuine units. If the brand has no registered trademark, start registration: registered marks can be reported without the mandatory test buy, though Amazon still recommends one.

## Cause

In the US, Canada and South Africa stores, a brand without a registered trademark can report only under common law, and a common-law report requires a completed test buy with its order ID. The agency asked for a test buy to photograph the unit, but the hijacker's offer was removed before the order could be placed, so no evidence was gathered. The thread implies, without stating it, that the brand's mark was unregistered. For registered marks Amazon recommends a test buy but does not require one.

## Fix

1. Place a test buy from the hijacker's offer as soon as it appears and keep the order ID.
2. Photograph the received unit, packaging and labels and compare them with the genuine product.
3. File Report a Violation with the order ID and photos only if the test buy shows the goods infringe the trademark. Choose Trademark infringement, and choose Product is counterfeit only when the unit is confirmed counterfeit. A seller offering genuine units cannot be reported this way. Operator approval before submitting.
4. If the brand relies on an unregistered mark, start a trademark registration so later reports do not depend on a test buy.

## Verify

The hijacker's offer no longer appears on the listing's offers page. The thread did not record a report status in Brand Registry.

## Stop before

Stop before any submission, appeal, case send, refund, price change or listing change unless the operator approved that exact action.
