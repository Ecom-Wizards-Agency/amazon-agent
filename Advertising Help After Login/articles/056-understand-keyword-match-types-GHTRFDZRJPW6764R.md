# Understand keyword match types

Learn about keyword match types, and your ability to adjust which shopping queries your ads appear.

Updated on Jul 29, 2026

Was this video helpful? Select feedback

Match types allow you to fine-tune which shopping queries your ads are eligible to show against. When a customer search term is not available, Amazon will infer a search term from the context that best matches your keywords and show your ads in relevant placements. For each keyword you add, you must also select a match type to fine-tune which searches trigger your ads. In Sponsored Products automatic targeting campaigns, you'll use targeting groups instead of match types for the same purpose.

**Note:**Once the campaign is created, you won't be able to change the match types you initially selected for your campaign. However, you can add keywords with different match types while your campaign is running.

## Manual targeting match types

In manual targeting campaigns, you have 3 options. They all account for plurals, misspellings, and translations. If you set multiple match types with different bids for the same keyword, then the match type with the highest bid will be used for any matching shopping query.

- **Broad match:**offers your ad broad exposure to customer shopping queries. A shopping query can contain the keyword terms in any order. It may include singulars, plurals, variations, synonyms, and related terms as determined by the meaning of the keyword and the context of the advertised products. The keyword itself may not be contained in the customer shopping query. Example,: the keyword “sneakers” may match customer shopping queries such as “canvas sneakers,” “sneakers,” “basketball shoes,” “athletic shoes,” “cleats,” “trainers,” or “foam runners.”
- **Phrase match:** your ad is shown when your keyword phrase or sequence of words, as well as their close variations like misspellings, plural forms, acronyms, translations appear in shopper queries. While Phrase match is more restrictive than broad match it can also lead to more relevant traffic, helping you strike a balance between precision and reach. Example: a phrase match keyword “curtain rod” can trigger your ad for searches such as “extra long curtain rods” or “curtain rods for bedroom.”
- **Exact match:**your ad is shown when your keyword or sequence of words exactly match shopper queries as well as their close variations like misspellings, plural forms, acronyms, translations. Exact match is the most restrictive match type, but can be more relevant to a shopper query. Example: if your exact match keyword is "outside lights," your ad can still appear when a customer searches for "outdoor lights".

**Tip:** Start with a broad match to measure where your ads perform the best. To do so, review either the keyword metrics in the Keywords tab of the campaign manager, or the targeting report. Review which keywords and search terms are performing best. Once you complete your review, you can change your bids or create a more concise group of keywords to optimize your campaigns and reach your goals. Learn more about Performance metrics and Reports.

## Broad match modifiers (Sponsored Brands)

In **Sponsored Brands**, you can add broad match modifiers to indicate words that must appear in the customers' shopping queries for your ad. Add a broad match modifier by adding a plus symbol "+" before the keyword. For example, if you use the keyword "+kids shoes" with a broad match modifier, then the ad will match only the searches that contain the word "kids." The ad may match to "kids sneakers" or “running shoes for kids” but they won’t match any shopping query that doesn’t contain the word "kids," such as “sneakers” or “running shoes.”

## Automatic targeting groups (Sponsored Products)

Automatic targeting is available only for Sponsored Products. After you create a Sponsored Products campaign with automatic targeting, you can see the targeting options in the campaign manager and adjust them to meet your campaign objectives. For example, you can increase your bid for a single strategy, or turn on or off a strategy all together.

- **Close match:** We'll show your ad to customers who use search terms closely related to your products. If your product is "Doppler 400-count Cotton Sheets," we'll show an ad when shoppers use search terms like "cotton sheets," "400-count sheets," "soft cotton sheets," "400TC bedsheets," and "100% cotton sheets 400 thread count."
- **Loose match:** We'll show your ad to customers who use search terms loosely related to your products. If your product is “Doppler 400-count Cotton Sheets,” we'll show an ad when shoppers use search terms like "bed sheets," "bath sheets," and "bath towels."
- **Substitutes:** We'll show your ad to customers who view detail pages of products similar to yours. If your product is "Doppler 400-count Cotton Sheets," we'll show an ad on product detail pages that include "300-count Cotton sheets" and "queen 400-count Sheets."
- **Complements:** We'll show your ad to customers who view the detail pages of products that complement your product. If your product is "Doppler 400-count Cotton sheets," we'll show an ad on product detail pages that include "queen quilt" and "feather pillows."

## Negative keyword match types

- **Negative phrase:** Ads don't show on shopping queries that contain the complete phrase or close variations. There’s a maximum limit of 4 words per negative keyword and 80 characters.
- **Negative exact:** Ads don't show on shopping queries that contain the exact phrase or close variation. There’s a maximum limit of 10 words per negative keyword and 80 characters.
