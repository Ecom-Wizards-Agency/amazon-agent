# Understand campaign spend

Learn about advertising billing, credit limits, billing frequency, and invoices.

Updated on May 13, 2026

## What's campaign spend?

Campaign spend is the total amount you've been charged for clicks on your sponsored ads. Sponsored ads use a cost-per-click model, which means you only pay when a customer clicks your ad, and you're never billed more than once per click.

Because our traffic quality systems scan each click for up to 72 hours after it occurs, the amount on your invoice may differ from what you see in the campaign manager. To see the exact amount you've been charged, refer to your invoice. The following terms describe how your charges are tracked on the **Billing** page:

- **Cost**: The total amount you've spent on clicks. This can fluctuate for up to 72 hours after a click due to traffic validation.
- **Total amount billed**: The total amount you owe at the end of a billing cycle, including all unpaid and outstanding charges.
- **Accumulating charges**: The total amount you owe at any given moment. This updates every time a transaction occurs.

**Tip**: Compare your budget to your actual cost to ensure that your budgets have enough margin and aren’t regularly exceeded. Exceeding your budget will pause your ads and potentially cause missed sales.

## Campaign spend vs total amount billed

The cost column in the campaign manager only shows the total click charges. Your invoice includes adjustments for invalid traffic, taxes, and promotions. Your invoice may also include creator commissions and promotional credit deductions that don't appear in the campaign manager cost column.

While 90% of detected invalid traffic (IVT) is removed in real time, traffic quality systems may take up to 72 hours to remove 100% of IVT. For this reason, you may see a discrepancy between the campaign manager and your invoice if you're looking at the cost from the last 3 days.

**Example:** An advertising invoice closes at the end of March and, due to the click validation process, there may be delayed clicks reported until the first days of April. Those clicks are still attributed to March. Since the invoice for March has already closed, those delayed clicks can be added to the advertising invoice for April, while the campaign manager still displays those clicks in March.

## Delayed transactions

In addition to traffic validation, billing system processing delays can cause charges from one month to appear on the next month's invoice. This is separate from invalid traffic removal. It occurs when transactions are processed after the billing cycle closes.

**Example:** Your campaign runs in November and a small number of charges are processed after the November invoice closes. Those charges appear on your December invoice instead. The campaign manager still attributes the clicks to November.

**Note:** Both traffic validation and delayed transactions can cause your invoice total to differ from the campaign manager. If you notice a discrepancy, compare your invoice line items to the campaign manager cost for the same date range.

## Seller Central data vs total amount billed

If you pay for advertising with your seller account, your settlement period may not match up with your advertising billing cycle. 1 invoice may cover multiple settlement periods, depending on the cadence of your settlements and your credit limit.
