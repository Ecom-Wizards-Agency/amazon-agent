# Set up keyword targeting

Learn how to add keywords to your campaigns and choose match types to control when your ads appear in customer searches.

Updated on Jul 29, 2026

## What is keyword targeting?

**Keyword targeting** allows you to choose keywords to drive additional product discovery and sales. It's effective for showing your ads where customers are most likely to discover and buy your products. Use this strategy when:

- You know the search terms that customers use to search for products similar to yours.
- You want the ability to set bids for individual keywords to have more control over your targeting and spend.

When a customer search term is not available, Amazon will infer a search term from the context that best matches your keywords and show your ads in relevant placements.

In **Sponsored Brands** campaigns using the **Reserve share of voice (SOV)**goal, you also have the option to reserve branded keywords. This gives you a fixed, upfront price to display your ads in the top of search placement for those keywords.

## Add new keywords to an existing campaign

Return to a campaign you set up previously to add or edit keywords:

1. In the navigation side menu, click **Campaigns**
2. Select desired campaign.
3. Select desired ad group.
4. Click **Targeting** in the left-hand menu.
5. Click **Add keywords**.
6. Choose your keywords and match types. Add at least 25 keywords in your campaigns and ad group. When using multiple match types (broad, phrase, and exact match) for the same keyword, set different bids for each match type. Use branded terms (brand/ product name) and target similar product category keywords to expand your campaign's reach to customers. Consider specificity. Some keywords may not receive many impressions because they aren't common shopping queries. But if a keyword is very specific, it may have low impressions. Example: For dog shampoo, "Yorkie shampoo" may yield fewer impressions but higher conversions than "shampoo" Include complementary keywords, like "digital camera" for memory cards. Replace low-performing keywords and increase bids on high-performing keywords using insights from the search terms report. In Sponsored products, use the **Filter by** drop-down to choose between: Clicks or Orders. This allows for keyword recommendations to be prioritized by either click volume or order volume depending on your choice. With each recommended keyword review, the suggested bids, metrics of impression share, and impression rank can help you select bids and surface new keywords. Use suggested keyword groups to discover relevant keywords. You only need to provide 1 bid value for each Keyword Group you add. Keyword groups automatically update your targeting based on evolving shopping behaviors, helping to ensure that your ads remain relevant. Leverage dynamic up and down bidding together with Keyword Groups to adjust your bids automatically based on varying shopper responses and performance across the various keywords within a group.
7. Browse suggested keywords, search, or upload a list. Click **Add** next to each keyword you want to include.
8. Click **Save**.

## What are keyword match types?

For each keyword you add, you must also select a match type to fine-tune which searches trigger your ads. With manual targeting campaigns you have three options. They all account for plurals, misspellings, and translations.

- **Broad**: Contains all the keywords in any order and includes related keywords.
- **Phrase**: Contains the exact phrase or sequence of keywords.
- **Exact**: Exactly matches the keyword or sequence of keywords.

In **Sponsored Brands** campaigns, phrase match also allows your ad to show on searches that have similar meaning or intent as your keyword, even if the exact wording differs. For example, a phrase match keyword "curtain rods" can trigger your ad when a customer searches "pole for curtains for bedroom".

## Monitor keyword performance

Once your campaign has been running for 2 weeks, use the search term impression share (SIS) report to check which words are performing best and which search terms generate the best traffic and clicks. Check the suggested bid and suggested bid range for ads that have won impressions over the past 7 days for guidance.

Use Impression share (IS) and Impression rank (IR) to understand your impression coverage and help you decide how much to bid.

- **Impression share (IS)** is the percentage of impressions that you're getting on this term relative to all the impressions it's generating. An IS of 0% means that you haven't received any impressions on the term.
- **Impression rank (IR)** is where your IS stands relative to other advertisers' share of impressions for the same term. Use these to understand your impression coverage and help you decide how much to bid.
